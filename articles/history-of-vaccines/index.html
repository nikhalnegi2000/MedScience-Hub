History of Vaccines

From Edward Jenner's discovery in 1796 to modern vaccines.

Vaccines are among the most important discoveries in medical science. They have helped prevent serious diseases such as smallpox, polio, measles and tetanus.

A vaccine trains the body's immune system to recognize and fight a particular disease. Vaccination has transformed public health and saved countless lives worldwide.

1. How Did Vaccination Begin?

Before modern vaccines existed, some societies practised variolation, exposing people to smallpox material in an attempt to protect them against the disease. This method carried serious risks.

  • Before the 1700s: Variolation was practised in several parts of the world.
  • 1796: British physician Edward Jenner demonstrated that exposure to cowpox could protect against smallpox.
  • 1798: Jenner published his findings, helping establish vaccination as a scientific method of prevention.

The word vaccine comes from the Latin word vacca, meaning cow.

2. Important Milestones in Vaccine History

1879–1885: Louis Pasteur

Louis Pasteur helped advance laboratory-based vaccine development. His research included vaccines for chicken cholera, anthrax and rabies. In 1885, rabies vaccination was used to treat Joseph Meister after a rabid dog bit him.

1955: Polio Vaccine

Jonas Salk developed an inactivated polio vaccine that was licensed in 1955. Albert Sabin later developed an oral polio vaccine, helping make large-scale vaccination campaigns easier.

1967–1980: Smallpox Eradication

The World Health Organization intensified its global smallpox eradication campaign in 1967. On 8 May 1980, smallpox was officially declared eradicated worldwide.

1974: Expanded Programme on Immunization

The WHO established its Expanded Programme on Immunization to increase protection against major vaccine-preventable diseases, particularly among children.

2020: COVID-19 Vaccines

COVID-19 vaccines were developed and deployed at unprecedented speed. Some vaccines use mRNA technology to help the immune system recognize a target protein. The first COVID-19 vaccine doses were administered in December 2020.

4. History of Vaccination in India

India has played an important role in vaccination through national immunization programmes and vaccine manufacturing.

  • 19th century: Smallpox vaccination spread in India.
  • 1978: India launched its Expanded Programme on Immunization.
  • 1985: The programme was expanded and renamed the Universal Immunization Programme (UIP).
  • 1995: The Pulse Polio Immunization programme began.
  • 2021: India began administering COVID-19 vaccines on 16 January.
  • Today: Routine immunization protects children and pregnant women against multiple vaccine-preventable diseases.

5. How Have Vaccines Changed Over Time?

Early Vaccines

Early vaccination developed from observations of natural infections, such as cowpox protection against smallpox.

Laboratory Vaccines

Scientists developed weakened or inactivated organisms and vaccines made from specific parts of pathogens.

Modern Vaccines

Genetic engineering and technologies such as mRNA have expanded the methods available for vaccine development.

Conclusion

The history of vaccines demonstrates how scientific research and international cooperation have transformed medicine. From Jenner's smallpox vaccine in 1796 to modern vaccines, vaccination has helped prevent illness, reduce deaths and eradicate smallpox.
